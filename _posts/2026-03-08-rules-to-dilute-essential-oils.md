## Introduction

Trying a new skincare product can be exciting, but even products marketed as *“gentle,” “natural,” “hypoallergenic,”* or *“for sensitive skin”* may cause irritation or an allergic reaction in some people.

Testing a new product on a small area of skin before using it more broadly may help identify some potential reactions.

However, no at-home test can guarantee that a product will not cause irritation, an allergy, a breakout, or another reaction later.

This guide provides general information about testing new skincare products and recognizing possible signs of irritation.

**Always read and follow the directions, warnings, and precautions provided by the product manufacturer.**

---

## What Is an At-Home Skincare Product Test?

An at-home skincare product test involves trying a product on a small area of skin before applying it more widely.

You can watch the area for signs such as:

- Redness
- Itching
- Burning or stinging
- Swelling
- Blistering
- Rash or other unusual irritation

Testing a small area first may help you notice a reaction before using the product over a larger area.

However, a successful test does **not** guarantee that you will tolerate the product with continued or broader use.

> **Important:** An informal at-home product test is not the same as diagnostic **patch testing** performed by a dermatologist, allergist, or other qualified healthcare professional. Medical patch testing is used to help identify specific causes of allergic contact dermatitis.

---

## Who May Want to Be Especially Cautious?

Testing a new skincare product may be particularly useful if you:

- Have sensitive or easily irritated skin
- Have reacted to skincare or cosmetic products in the past
- Are trying a product containing ingredients that may be irritating to your skin
- Are introducing several unfamiliar ingredients into your routine
- Are uncertain how your skin will respond to a product

If you have eczema, rosacea, dermatitis, known allergies, another skin condition, or a history of significant reactions, consider asking a qualified healthcare professional for guidance before trying unfamiliar products.

---

## Before Testing a New Product

Before applying the product, check its packaging and instructions.

Look for:

- Directions for use
- Ingredient information
- Warnings or precautions
- Instructions for a manufacturer-recommended patch or sensitivity test
- Directions about how long the product should remain on the skin
- Directions about how frequently
